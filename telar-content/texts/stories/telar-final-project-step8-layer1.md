One of the most noticeable features of the Nueva Coronica is Guaman Poma's way of combining handwritten alphabetic text and drawings. This is important to change and continuity because colonialism introduced and expanded European alphabetic writing in the Andes, but visual communication continued to play an important role. Guaman Poma did not have to choose between communicating through words or images, he instead brought them together on the pages of the same manuscript. His drawings can show people, clothing, social positions, actions, and relationships, while the written text provides additional explanation. The coexistence of these forms demonstrates that changes in writing do not necessarily mean that previous ways of communicating immediately disappear. Instead, different forms can overlap and influence one another. The Nueva Corónica therefore represents a moment of transformation in which Guaman Poma works within a colonial alphabetic tradition while continuing to rely heavily on visual organization to communicate his understanding of Andean society.
