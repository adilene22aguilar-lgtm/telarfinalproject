In this section the Khipus were able to be transmitted among the Inca's society solely only through a specially long trained group named "quipucamayus." These Incas were specialized at a very young age in order to be responsible for maintaining, reading, and interpreting the type of knots and arrangements that they were in the khipus. For example, Gracilaso expands that the "quipucamayus" were selected based on their ability and experience rather than being favored due to one's status. This role was significant in the Inca society as well due to the power that the "quipucamayus" held as they essentially organized the Inca government due to the things that they recorded. This included tributes, military service, births and deaths, supplies, and all other information that can count. This demonstrates how the "quipucamayus" not only counted but also organized how the Inca government maintained their information. To add on, Gracilaso also highlights how the quipucamayus were essentially named as the keepers of cultural memory and historians. Overall, the quipucamayus used their own memories to preserve the information stored in the Inca community as being able to interpret the Khipus was a privilege that was highly valued to maintain the Inca government.
