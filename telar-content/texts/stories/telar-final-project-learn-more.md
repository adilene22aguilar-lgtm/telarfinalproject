Khipus played a significant role for Inca's society as it didn't rely on the common alphabetic system. Instead it depended on quantitative and qualitative data from the khipus as a whole to transfer information. For instance, in Gracilaso "Royal Commentaries" Book 6 it states, "Each threads of various colors, some were of a single hue, others of two, others
of three or more, for single or mixed colors all had separate significances." This highlights the significance of Khipus as they were more than a physical object but also a shared communication system among the Inca empire. 

To add on, throughout the Inca society it was an incredible widespread system that shared messages throughout the empire. There were also runners named "chasquis" that carried verbal messages as another form of communication in the Inca society. As a whole the object Khipus holds a monumental importance to the Inca as it not only provided a way for the empire to communicate through qualitative data but also a form of organization that no other empire in history has thought of following.
